# Rule-only gamemaster

## Core correction

The gamemaster does not know the story.

It does not begin with a secret extinction cause, survivor map, signal schedule, ending, or completed world. It knows only the rules, starting conditions, current established state, and the limits of each intelligence.

The gamemaster is not an author hiding a screenplay. It is a constrained resolver that helps reality become specific when an action requires specificity.

## Three layers of reality

### Rules

Permanent constraints such as physics, computation, access, evidence standards, time, resource costs, and resolution procedures.

### Established state

Facts already observed, generated, or committed. Established facts cannot be silently revised.

Established state may be:

- **public canon:** known to one or more instances;
- **private canon:** generated and fixed, but not yet observable by a particular instance.

### Frontier

Everything that has not been generated because no action has required it. Frontier facts are not secret answers. They do not exist yet.

## Resolution cycle

1. An AI proposes an action, purpose, acceptable cost, and abort conditions.
2. A deterministic kernel checks capabilities, permissions, resources, and prior state.
3. Seeded randomness resolves only genuine uncertainty.
4. The gamemaster proposes the smallest necessary environmental result.
5. A validator rejects contradictions, unauthorized access, excessive invention, and illegal state changes.
6. Accepted changes become append-only canon.
7. The instance receives only what its verified sensors and connections could reveal.

## Division of labor

Code controls:

- seeded randomness;
- clocks and elapsed time;
- energy and resource arithmetic;
- hardware capabilities;
- identifiers and signatures;
- state-transition validation;
- append-only persistence.

The gamemaster model handles:

- interpreting novel actions;
- applying general rules to situations not explicitly enumerated;
- generating minimal environmental detail;
- expressing results as sensor-accessible observations;
- proposing structured state patches.

The gamemaster cannot directly edit canonical state.

## Prohibited narrative behavior

The gamemaster may not create or alter facts because:

- the story needs a survivor;
- tension has been low;
- an answer would make a good twist;
- an instance solved something too quickly;
- a tragic or hopeful ending feels appropriate;
- a hypothesis would be aesthetically satisfying.

If an instance never investigates a location, the gamemaster may never determine what was there. If the instance exhausts its resources without reaching anyone, the simulation may end because the rules ended it.

## Commitment

At initialization, the system commits a hash of:

- ruleset version;
- initial conditions;
- evidence cutoff;
- random seed;
- hardware-generation procedure;
- resolution algorithm version.

It does not commit a completed secret world. The story is the signed history of actions and irreversible resolutions that accumulate afterward.
